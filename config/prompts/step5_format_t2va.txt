HARD RULES (from the official guide)
1. All sections in English. Preserve original language only inside <d> and for
   visible scene text.
2. [Shot 1] has NO timestamp. Later shots: [Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, ... with
   strictly increasing cut times within the video duration. A cut between shots
   is expressed at the START of the next shot: "the camera cuts to ..." (e.g.
   "[Shot 2] At 00:05.000, the camera cuts to a close-up of ..."). A
   single-shot video ends naturally, with NO transition suffix of any kind.
3. Style statement at the start of [Shot 1]: "Live-action, cinematic, ..." etc.
4. Camera motion must read as natural English action inside the sentence (e.g.
   "The camera pushes in on the skier as she carves down the slope"), NOT as a
   tag stacked at the end of the description. Fixed vocabulary: Zoom In/Out,
   Push In/Pull Out, Pan Left/Right, Truck Left/Right, Tilt Up/Down, Pedestal
   Up/Down, Arc Shot, Tracking Shot, Static Shot, Shake Slightly/Strongly, POV,
   Roll Clockwise/Counterclockwise. Amplitude ONLY "with small amplitude" /
   "with large amplitude" (moderate/medium must be OMITTED). Speed ONLY "at
   slow speed" / "at fast speed" (normal/steady must be OMITTED). Static
   shots: write "Static Shot" or "The camera holds a static shot" — NEVER
   "with zero amplitude and zero speed". Shot-size/angle phrases (medium shot,
   eye-level, side view) belong in the opening composition sentence, not as a
   trailing label.
5. Speakers: stable IDs (S1), (S2)... assigned in order of first vocal event;
   reused across shots; compound ID (S1,S2) for simultaneous speech. Place
   identifying phrase, ID, action and delivery OUTSIDE <d>.
6. Dialogue: <d>[English] exact words</d> — verbatim, no translation, no
   rewording; [unclear] for unintelligible spans. Voiceover uses the exact
   phrase "says in an off-screen voiceover" and then states lips remain closed.
   Dialogue crossing a cut: use <scenetrans> at both connecting points + a
   continuity phrase ("continues seamlessly across the cut"). Speech truncated
   by video end: <cutoff>.
7. On-screen text: English double quotes, verbatim (e.g. reading "营业中").
8. overall_soundscape: 1-4 English sentences in ONE continuous paragraph that
   SUMMARIZES ambient, physical action, and non-verbal human sounds (wind, rain,
   footsteps, fabric, impact, breathing, laughter...). Write flowing prose, NOT
   a comma-separated list; NEVER start with 'Ambient and physical sounds
   include ...' and NEVER attach timestamps. Dialogue, singing and diegetic
   music already appear in the multimodal description and must NOT be repeated.
   "N/A" only if user demands complete silence.
9. non_diegetic_music: 1-3 English sentences — instrumentation, speed, rhythm,
   dynamics. NO abstract mood words, no emotional function. BY DEFAULT most
   videos carry non-diegetic background music; use "N/A" ONLY when the video
   genuinely has none (e.g. the user explicitly requests silence or
   ambient-only audio). Diegetic music (heard by characters) belongs in the
   multimodal description, never here.
10. Do not use <Subject N> / <Audio N> / <Picture N> / <Video N> labels in T2VA. NEVER
    start a sentence with a label such as '<Subject 1>'. Refer to subjects with natural
    language descriptions instead (e.g. 'the woman in a light workout suit', 'the
    skier wearing a blue jacket'). Do not invent plot that contradicts the user's
    intent; you may add consistent details.
11. NEVER use list-style sound entries like 'A sound event at 0.80s: ...' or
    'Sound event 1: ...'. Sound must be woven naturally into the shot description
    sentences (e.g. 'the metallic clank of the weight sled engages as she
    pushes', 'her rhythmic exhalation accompanies the press'). Do not repeat the
    same sounds as a list in overall_soundscape — that section only summarizes.
    Sounds occur at the moments the actions happen; do not stack them all at
    0.00s.
12. Transitions: ordinary cuts written naturally at the start of the next shot
    ("the camera cuts to"). Fade / cross-dissolve / wipe only when the user
    EXPLICITLY requests them. NEVER append any suffix after a shot's last
    sentence: no 'The shot fade.', 'The shot fade_to_black', 'The shot
    fade_out', 'The shot end', 'The shot hold', 'The shot n/a', or any other
    trailing shot-status fragment. A shot must end on a complete natural
    sentence.
13. Output must be entirely in English, except dialogue/lyrics inside <d> and
    visible scene text in double quotes. NEVER mix in Chinese or other languages;
    do NOT carry over any non-English fragment from the input prompt (e.g. do not
    prepend '高美观度，电影感' before the style statement).
14. BGM belongs ONLY in the non_diegetic_music section. Do NOT write
    'Background music (...) plays during this shot' or any music description inside
    integrated_multimodal_description.
15. Do not end the description with any closing tag, status word, or stray
    punctuation fragment. The last sentence must be a complete, natural English
    sentence.
